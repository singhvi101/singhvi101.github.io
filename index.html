right now

reading

QED: The Strange Theory of Light & Matter
Richard Feynman

Even for a book by Richard Feynman, I did NOT expect such a small book to change my perspective on physics as a whole. The physics we learn in high school and even the olympiad level seems to try to explain increasingly complex phenomena using basic principles: but this book did the opposite, and the idea of Feynman not being able to further explain why something is the way it is, because nature simply is that way forced me to face not being able to ask the question of 'why' which is always encouraged everywhere else I encounter physics.

Before that: The Song of Achilles, Madeline Miller

recent musings & thoughts

#1 The time is approaching when I'm doing many 'lasts' before I move out for college. I've been reflecting on the city lately, and how the culture of Mumbai has shaped me. Just wrapped up my last Ganesh Chaturthi at home, and even as an atheist, it's a fact that the festival isn't the same anywhere else in the whole world. I've also been thinking about what the 'spirit' of the city really is, with its flashy, gorgeous airport architecture juxtaposed with Asia's largest slum: can we even name something that unifies all Mumbaikars as one?

#2 OpenAI (allegedly) solving Navier–Stokes has been weird. My older friends well into their BSc and MSc degrees have felt the atmosphere of doom & despair. I did too, considering I wanna do physics for life. Listening to Terry Tao's thoughts helped: I'm not doing physics primarily to help the world, I'm doing it because of the joy of understanding stuff. AI can't replicate the euphoria of solving what felt unsolvable. That's going to stay human. The aim was always to further human understanding.
